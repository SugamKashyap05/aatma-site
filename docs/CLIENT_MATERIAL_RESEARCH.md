## 1. Item 3 — *The Assam Tribune*, 18 November 2024
**Article:** "Prospects of Assam's agarwood industry" — by PJ Vijayakar
**Standalone feature article** (no photo, decorative tree graphic only)

### Facts asserted (all attributable to this article)

**Conservation status**
- Agarwood is **critically endangered** on the IUCN Red List
- Listed under **CITES Appendix II** — export permitted only if it does not harm species survival
- Government of India, via MoEFCC, commissioned a **Non-detriment Findings (NDF) study**
- The NDF report was submitted by the **Botanical Survey of India in February 2024**

**Tree population (per the NDF report)**
- Assam holds **over 82% of India's agarwood trees** — **11.437 crore** trees outside forest areas
- **Golaghat district: 4.5 crore** (highest concentration in the state)
- Other key districts: **Jorhat, Sivasagar, Karimganj, Cachar**
- Tripura is second nationally with **1.515 crore**

**Policy framework**
- **January 2023** — Assam notified the *Assam Wood-Based Industries (Promotion & Development) Rules, 2022* (WBI Rules) **and** the *Assam Trees Outside Forest (Sustainable Management) Rules, 2022* (ToF Rules)
- ToF Rules regulate the **supply side**; WBI Rules stimulate **demand**
- Agar listed as an **exempt species (Schedule-III)** — simplifies harvest and transport within Assam
- Previously agarwood distillation units were classed like sawmills/plywood mills and required relocation to industrial estates
- Under the new framework they are **secondary wood-based industries** requiring only **simple online registration, valid five years**
- **Assam Agarwood Promotion Policy, 2020** also notified

**Institutional infrastructure**
- **Assam Agar International Trade Centre** to be established in **Golaghat district**
- Aims to eliminate middlemen and give farmers/traders direct access to national and international buyers

**Market data**
- 2018–2022 global agarwood chips market grew at **7.7% CAGR**, reaching **$44.05 billion** (~Rs 3,52,405.6 crore) by 2023
- Projected **$64 billion** (~Rs 5,12,000 crore) by 2029
- High-quality agarwood ~**Rs 1 lakh per kg** internationally; agar oil up to **Rs 80,000 per kg**
- Domestic chips **Rs 10,000–3 lakh per kg**; agar oil **Rs 6,000–17,500 per tola (11.66 gm)**

**Trade statistics (2017–2021)**
- India imported **>142 tonnes** agarwood, re-exported **141 tonnes**
- Eastern India (esp. Assam) accounted for **74% of imports** and **92% of re-exports**

**Export quota (per NDF study)**
- Assam secured **79% of the country's export quota** for agarwood and agar oil
- **1.194 lakh kg** agarwood chips per annum
- **5,560 kg** agar oil per annum

**Stated problem**
- Industry response described as **tepid**; many existing units in Assam **yet to register** under the new rules
- Consequence: **tax evasion** and **reduced profits** for farmers and traders

## 2. Item 4 — *The Assam Tribune*, 2 August 2023 (page 6)
**Article:** "Wood-based industries: Winds of change soon" — by PJ Vijayakar
**Same page also carries an unrelated editorial, "River dredging"** — not AATMA material, ignore.

### Facts asserted

**Wood as a resource**
- Wood is climate-friendly, carbon-neutral, a substitute for plastic
- ~**half the dry weight of wood is carbon**, sequestered in wood products

**Legal history**
- The WBI sector changed due to Supreme Court orders in **Godavarman Thirumulpad v. Union of India**
- Assam notified the *WBI (Establishment & Regulation) Rules 2000* pursuant to that order

**Industry counts (primary WBIs)**
- **Pre-Godavarman: 588** primary WBIs — 502 saw mills, 57 plywood mills, 27 veneer mills, 2 ply-cum-veneer mills
- **Post: reduced by 66%** → **~206 primary WBIs** now functional in Assam

**Agarwood units under the 2000 regime**
- **~1,500** agarwood-based units, "basically cottage industries"
- Required to shift to notified Industrial Estates and obtain licence
- Owners were **not willing to relocate** → the agarwood industry "suffered a major setback"

**The 2022 Rules**
- Notified under **Chief Minister Himanta Biswa Sarma**
- Paradigm shift: forest department moves **from regulator to facilitator**
- Two categories: units requiring **licence** vs not, based on machinery and raw material
- Categories: **primary, secondary, composite** units
- **Only primary WBIs** need a licence and must be in notified Industrial Estates
- **Secondary WBIs** (agarwood, katha, furniture) require **registration only** — need not be in Industrial Estates
- Agarwood units **de-licensed** and classed as secondary WBIs
- Registration enables a **legal procurement certificate** for exports under CITES

**Time limits**
- Licence/registration **valid five years**
- Procedure **fully online**, drastically reducing human interface
- Time limits prescribed at intermediate levels; applications **auto-advance** if a level exceeds its limit
- **Max 74 days** for fresh licence or renewal (primary WBIs) per 5-year period
- **Max 62 days** for fresh registration or renewal (secondary WBIs) per 5-year period

**Other**
- **Captive plantations** by WBIs envisaged — twin objectives of assured raw material supply and assured farmer income
- Integration of WBIs and tree farming expected to create a positive business ecosystem
- Assam WBIs **lag in technology and skill** → inefficient use of raw materials, energy, labour
- Big-ticket investments planned to close that gap
- Government keen to notify a **separate investment promotion policy** for WBIs including individual artisans
- A **web portal** for implementing the new rules was "slated to be launched very soon"

*The Assam Gazette, Extraordinary — No. 1, Dispur, Monday, 2nd January, 2023 (12th Pausa, 1944 S.E.). GOVERNMENT OF ASSAM, ORDERS BY THE GOVERNOR, ENVIRONMENT AND FOREST DEPARTMENT, NOTIFICATION. 25 pages. **No text layer — pure scanned images.***

- Short title: **The Assam Trees Outside Forest (Sustainable Management) rules, 2022**
- Comes into force on publication in the Official Gazette

**Rule 2 definitions verified verbatim**
- "Captive Plantation" — a plantation scheme covering a continuous patch of **10 (Ten) hectares or more**, of trees as specified under sub-section (2) of section 3 of the Regulation, raised by WB industrial unit(s) and/or other private entities on non-forest land
- "Certificate of Origin" — a certificate/document testifying origin, issued by the DFO having jurisdiction over where the raw material originated, to owners/legal title holders/legal assigns, for traceability
- "Department" = Environment and Forest department of Government of Assam
- "Government" = Government of Assam
- "Forest" includes: (i) Reserved Forest or Protected Area under the Wildlife Protection Act 1972, or any area constituted as forest under any statute; (ii) any area recorded as "Forest" in Government records; (iii) **a continuous patch of 10 hectares or more having not less than 200 naturally grown trees per hectare on average**
- "Non-forest land" = an area which is not "forest" per clause (e) of rule 2
- "Owner" = legal title-holder (individual/firm/company/trust/society/body corporate) holding land in own name and/or with a valid lease deed
- "Plantation" = (i) a plantation scheme covering a continuous patch of **0.1 hectare or more** of "trees" as defined under sub-section (2) of section 3 of the Regulation, grown in a single year or over multiple years, on non-forest land or community land; (ii) excludes species covered under sub-rule (1) of **rule 5** if planted under any plantation scheme

> **These two hectare thresholds (10 ha for "forest", 0.1 ha for a "plantation") are the legal crux of the ToF Rules and are precise, quotable, and absent from the current site.**
