## 1. Agar in Assamese culture

- The agar tree (*sanchi*) is "one of the precious gifts of nature", deeply associated with the culture of Assam and North East India since ancient times.
- **Bark of the agar tree, called *sanchi paat*, was used for writing scriptures.**
- The renowned Vaishnav saints of Assam — **Srimanta Sankar Dev, Madhav Deb and Damudar Deb** — used *sanchipat* for writing scriptures.
- Since then, Assamese people of Upper Assam planted the *sanchi* plant in their homesteads, which became part of ritual.
- **Agaru is an inseparable *puja* article of Hindus since ancient times.**
- Botanical classification: ***Aquilaria***, family **Thymelaeaceae**.
- **Agarwood chips** (black resinous wood) and **agar oil** (essential oil / attar) are the most useful and oldest raw materials for perfume.
- Agar is therefore a highly commercial plant.

## 2. About the trade

- The trade is currently spread across the **Indian sub-continent, the Middle East, European countries and South East Asia**.
- Due to the many-fold increase in perfume use in India in recent times, agar oil and its products are now highly demanded in both domestic and international markets for the perfumery industry.

## 3. The early trade, 1905–1970

| Period | Event |
|---|---|
| **1905–06** | Agarwood was found abundantly in the forests of the North Eastern region, and people of **Sylhet** (now in Bangladesh) used it for extracting oil |
| **Post-Independence** | Certain entrepreneurs of **Barpather** started an agarwood business in Barpather in their homesteads. They collected raw agarwood from the forest mahals of **Karbi Anglong and Naga Hills** |
| **Following years** | Visionary people of the **Hojai** area entered the trade and started small-scale distillation at Hojai |
| **A few years later** | The agar trade and distillery business also began in **Naharani and other parts of Jorhat and Sibsagar districts**, using agarwood harvested from their own homegrown trees |
| **Late 1960s** | A group from Hojai took leases of agar mahals in **NEFA and Bhutan**, collecting agar there and bringing it to Hojai |
| **Until 1970** | The Government of Assam leased agarwood mahals for a fixed period. By this time agar trade had developed as a **cottage industry in the unorganised sector** |
| **1970–1980s** | Entrepreneurs of **Hojai and Silchar** collected agarwood from the mahaldars of **Manipur State** (under Forest Transit Pass), processed and distilled for oil at Hojai and Silchar. Products were then taken to **Bombay (Mumbai)** under a *Lieu T.P.* issued by the Assam Forest Department for sale |

## 4. Far East discovery and the import era

- For a short while from **1970 to 1980**, the Assam trade survived on agar from Manipur, but **came to a standstill in 1982**.
- A sudden change came with the **discovery of agarwood abundantly in the Far East** — **Malaysia, Indonesia, Thailand, Cambodia**.
- Prominent Hojai business houses began importing agarwood from those countries under the Government of India's Exim Policy, paying heavy import duties.
- **Hojai had a good number of skilled agar processing workers and agar oil distilleries; employment was as high as 5,000 people.**
- Imported raw agarwood was **re-exported after processing** in their factories as finished products.

## 5. Formation of AATMA

- The trade, being a cottage-based industry in the unorganised sector, suffered many obstacles — largely from a lack of knowledge on the part of traders as well as law-enforcing agencies, plus undue and uncalled-for interference.
- It was felt by prominent Hojai traders **back in 1975–76** that some form of association or NGO was needed, to improve and expand the trade.
- **The All Assam Agar Traders and Agaroil Manufacturers' Association (AATMA) was formed in 1976**, under the presidency and secretaryship of:
  - **(L) Hazi Anfar Ali** — President
  - **(L) Hazi Samsul Islam** — Secretary
  - **(L) Binay Kumar Paul**
- **Head Office at Hojai**, duly registered vide **Reg. No. 664 of 1978–79**.
- Since then the Association continuously fought legally and by submitting representations to government to remove interference and hurdles.
- **In 2000, AATMA was reorganised** in a General Meeting at Hojai, with:
  - **Ataur Rahman** as **President**
  - **(L) Jamal Uddin Choudhury** as **General Secretary**
  - and an Executive body.

## 6. National seminar on agarwood plantation

- **November 2000** — AATMA organised a national-level seminar on *"Scope & Dimension of Agar (Aquilaria spp.) Plantation in North Eastern Region"* at the **Administrative Staff College, Guwahati**.
- Attended by several scientists from across India.
- **Presided over by Dr. P.K. Hajra, Ex-Director of the Botanical Survey of India.**
- Several scientific reports on agarwood, plantation and development submitted by participating scientists are incorporated in the **seminar proceedings**.
- The seminar was an **eye-opener** — entrepreneurs began commercial plantation on private land in **Assam, Nagaland, Tripura and Manipur**, where it proved a great success and is now a **major source of raw material** for the region's agar trade and industry.
- A survey conducted **jointly by AATMA and the Assam Government** reveals **more than a crore of agar trees in private cultivation** in the Upper Assam region and other parts of the state.
- Widespread private cultivation has also flourished in **Tripura and Nagaland**.
- **Lakhs of people are involved in agar cultivation and trade.**

## 7. The critical era, 2001–2016

### The 2000 Rules and the first representations

| Date | Event |
|---|---|
| **2000** | Government of Assam framed the *Assam Wood Based Industries (Establishment and Regulation) Rules, 2000*, classifying **agar oil extracting units as wood based industries**. This plunged agar growers and traders into a period of deep uncertainty |
| **26 June 2000** | AATMA representatives met the then Chief Minister of Assam, submitting a **memorandum urging Government to exclude agar oil extraction units** from the purview of the Rules — arguing traders sourced raw material from **private cultivation (patta land or homesteads)** and through **legal import from abroad**. The memorandum also requested the agar wood industry be officially declared an **agro-based industry**. **No concrete action was taken** |
| **2001** | AATMA submitted a fresh representation and memorandum on the same matter. Vigorously pursued multiple times |
| **December 2003** | At the request of the then Forest Minister, AATMA sponsored and participated in *"The Workshop on Agar Plantation and the Rule of Joint Forest Management Committees of Assam"*, held at **Rabindra Bhavan on 6 and 7 January 2004** |

### The import model and its collapse

- Until that point, agar processing units were not governed by the Rules. Instead they survived **by importing agarwood from abroad until 2005**.
- This arrangement required **verification of consignments by the concerned Divisional Forest Officer (DFO)** at both import and re-export, to ensure **CITES compliance**.
- **October 2005** — the then DFO of Hojai, **Shri Siv Kumar, IFS**, **denied the verification**. This was **unlawful and arbitrary**, allegedly based on the **verbal instruction from the then Forest Minister of Assam**.
- As a result, **around seven processing units suffered massive financial losses**, unable to re-export their raw and processed agarwood.
- This caused severe damage to the local industry and a **significant loss of foreign exchange earnings**.
- Finding no alternative, several prominent business houses **shifted operations to neighbouring states like Meghalaya, and overseas**.
- The prospects of a highly viable industry were **severely crippled and strangled within Assam**.

### The Central Empowered Committee recommendation — rejected

- As per **CCF(T) letter No. FGT39/WBI/Amand/SecondaryWBI/06 dated 01.03.2006**, the **Central Empowered Committee agreed to certain changes** in the 2000 Rules and **recommended incorporating the agarwood industries into Secondary Wood Based Industry**, which could be set up **outside designated Industrial Areas**.
- **Unfortunately, the Assam Cabinet did not approve the recommendation.**
- All AATMA efforts to streamline the trade did not succeed — attributed to "the deaf ear and negligence of the then Govt. of Assam."

### Continued struggle, 2005–2016

- AATMA office-bearers, though disheartened, did not sit idle. Through **2005–2016** they continued to meet the Chief Minister, Forest Minister and concerned forest department authorities, submitting memorandums to exclude the agar industry from the 2000 Rules.
- The Forest Department submitted several recommendations proposing to bring agar industries under **Secondary Wood Based Industry**, as approved by the Central High Power Committee.
- **These efforts did not yield the desired result** and the agar industry received no due recognition.

### Life on the ground

> "Because the agar industry was never officially legalised or exempted under these rigid rules — largely due to the persistent unwillingness of the then Forest Minister up until 2016 — the everyday reality for those on the ground became a nightmare. Routine transportation of agar wood, even when legally harvested from private cultivated lands, was treated as a criminal act. Business personnel and local cultivators faced relentless harassment from both legal authority and forest department officials. This hostile environment led to numerous unjustified seizures of cultivated agar wood, causing immense financial distress and systemic disruption for the traders and farmers struggling to keep this traditional industry alive."

- **24 August 2016** — an AATMA delegation led by **Humayun Chaudhury** met the then Forest Minister **Smt. Pramila Rani Brahma** at the **Hojai Forest Circuit House**. They submitted a memorandum on the challenges the industry faced under the 2000 Rules.
- She took a keen interest, and **at her behest a proposal was submitted to Government** to classify the agar wood industry as a **Secondary Wood Based Industry**, excluding it from the 2000 Rules.
- Subsequently, under her initiative, the Assam Forest Department organised a **National Conference on "Agarwood & Oil Industries: Prospect & Possibilities"**, held on **28 October 2016 at NEDFi House, Guwahati**.

## 8. Achievement — the 2022 Rules

| Date | Event |
|---|---|
| **19 July 2021** | An AATMA delegation, accompanied by **Mr. Sirajuddin Ajmal, MLA**, met the Chief Minister **Shri Himanta Biswa Sharma**, submitting a memorandum apprising him of the problems faced by agar industries over the previous **21 years** due to the 2000 Rules. The CM listened patiently, acknowledged the memorandum, and assured them he would work on resolving it |
| **12 January 2023** | True to his assurance, the CM and his government enacted the **Assam Wood Based Industries (Promotion & Development) Rules 2022** |

**Consequences of the 2022 Rules:**
- Agar Wood Industries are now declared **Secondary Wood Based Industries**
- They can **operate outside the Industrial Estate**
- The trade is successfully **liberalised and simplified** from the grip of strict laws
- These Rules will **pave the way for smooth trade and the overall development of plantations**
