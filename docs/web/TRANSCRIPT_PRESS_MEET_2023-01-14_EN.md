# PART 1 — Asom First broadcast (v1, 6 min 26 s)

## Speaker 1 — 0:01

"[**uncertain** — opening phrase garbled; likely a seasonal or ceremonial greeting, not "Bihu"] — good news has come for the agar industry. At last the Assam government has legalised the agar wood trade.

We thank Assam's Chief Minister Himanta Biswa Sarma. The All Assam Agarwood Trade and Agar Oil Manufacturers' Association is extremely happy.

On 12 January the Assam government issued a new notification, giving legal validity to the agar trade. By amending the Assam Wood Based Industries (Establishment and Regulation) Rules 2000, this notification gives legal standing to the enterprises dependent on agar wood. From now on the import of agar wood, and its export abroad, will become easier.

The new notification identifies micro, medium and large agar enterprises and provides a licensing arrangement, giving the state's small agar traders the opportunity to conduct their business according to their own capacity.

For this Gazette notification that legalises the agar trade, the Association expresses its thanks through this press meet. We are extremely happy.

Present at this press meet were AATMA's President **[name withheld]**, General Secretary **[name withheld]**, Editor **[name withheld]**, **[name withheld]**, and other office-bearers of AATMA."

## Speaker 2 — 1:20

"As you know, since the year 2000 — the All Assam Agarwood Traders and Agar Oil Manufacturers' Association — the Wood Based Industry Act that came in 2000 shut down the entire trade in Assam's North East, all the wood-related products there were closed.

And our trade rested on two raw materials. One was brought from abroad, and the other was wood produced by our own farmers — from their own homes, from their own production. That is how the industry was running at the time.

But our import-export trade ran well until 2005. The local *sanchi* trees — the trade made from *sanchi* trees — this trade became completely illegal under government policy.

But the government knew that this was a household item, not a forest product. So it was allowed to survive somehow. At their mercy, we have run this trade for so many years. Although officially we were not in this trade. Officially we had distanced ourselves from this trade for a long time.

The small and marginal people — the trade has been crushed. Production has become a local product. Within it, many goods are seized on the roads and at the ferries, by the police, the forest department, the customs. Much loss has been suffered. And the government has been the biggest loser of all.

The goods from which the Government of India and the Government of Assam would have collected GST, CST — at that time CST, ST — no tax at all was collected by the government. Because this was never legalised.

And since then — almost four [governments] in Assam: in 2000 the AGP government, after 2000 three Congress governments, then one BJP government. Roughly five Assam governments came and went. And with them, with them, with them, we tried again and again to make them understand.

Today I am very happy, and I tell you with joy: the 9 December notification — when it was notified that of Assam's **seventy-seven species**, seventy-seven types of wood are listed as national products. And the wood that grows in non-forest areas — meaning in the *mihadi* [homestead] plots, whichever of these seventy-seven species grows in *mihadi* plots — the trade in all of those has been exempted from the forest, for transportation purposes across the whole of Assam, so that nobody can arrest them.

The species that grow within five kilometres of a forest area — all kinds of wood are there, mango, jackfruit, everything. Because of this, for transportation, for doing business, no licence is required.

And the gazette notification of the tenth, the eleventh, the twelfth — the one that declared plywood factories, saw mills, veneer mills, everything illegal and shut them down in Assam."

## Speaker 2 — 9:34 *(delivered in English — original text, not translated)*

> **On 12 January 2023, AATMA takes this opportunity to publicly thank Dr. Himanta Vishwa Sharma, Honourable Chief Minister of Assam, for his visionary leadership and for taking this great initiative of bringing back the glory of agarwood-based industries.**
>
> **There are about 1,000 — more than 1,000 — agarwood-based industries in Assam, which are predominantly located in Golaghat, Sibsagar, Jorhat, Majuli, Nagaon, Karimganj, Cachar, Dima Hasao and Boroland, among others. These industrial units suffered setback due to the imposition of various legal restrictions for the operation of agarwood-based industries.**
>
> **AATMA expresses its pleasure over the repealing of the existing Assam Wood Based Industries (Establishment and Regulation) Rules 2000 and the notifying of the Assam Wood-Based Industries (Promotion and Development) Rules 2022.**
>
> **Major features of the Assam Wood-Based Industries (Promotion and Development) Rules 2022 — such as categorising agarwood-based industries as secondary wood-based industries, liberating the trade from the licence regime and associated restrictions, and simplifying the procedure for registration of agarwood-based industries by the Registration Committees at the level of the Conservator of Forests concerned — will immensely benefit the agarwood industry and provide the much-needed boost.**
>
> **The rules are made fully CITES compliant and pave the way for the revival of the agarwood industries and the import and re-export, as well as the export, of domestic agarwood products. With the notification of these rules, agarwood-based industries in Assam can function from their present locations after obtaining registration under the new rules, which is valid for five years.**
>
> **The categorisation of agarwood-based industries into micro, small, medium and large units based on the aggregate capacity of each unit provides the necessary impetus for small traders to conduct the trade in an affordable manner. AATMA firmly believes that all these provisions in the new rules will transform the agarwood trade into a vibrant industry.**
>
> **AATMA duly recognises the recent notification.**
