A BRIEF HISTORY OF AGAR
Agar Tree of Sanchi plant is one of the precious gift of nature and deeply associated with the
culture of Assam & North eastern India since ancient time . Bark of agar tree called sanchi paat
was used for writing scripture . The renown Vaishnab saints of Assam Srimanta  Sankar Dev ,
Madhav Deb and Damudar Deb used Sanchipat  for writing scriptures. Since than the
Assamese people of upper Assam planted Sanchi plant in their homestead which become a
part of  ritual . Agaru is an inseparable puja article  of  Hindus since ancient time .It belongs to
Aquilaria of family Thymeleaceae . Agarwood chips (Black resinous wood)and Agar Oil
(Essential Oil) Attar are most useful and oldest raw materials for perfume . Thus agar is a highly
commercial plant .
                                                        ABOUT THE TRADE
The trade in Agar is currently spread over the Indian sub-continent ,  the middle east ,European
countries and south east Asia . Due to many time increase use of perfume in India in recent
past Agar oil  and its product now highly demanded in the domestic as well as International
market for perfumery industries .
Agar wood was found abundantly in the  forests of North Eastern region and used by some
people of Sylhet (Now in Bangladesh) region for extracting oil  back in 1905-06.   After
Independence of India   certain entrepreneurs of Barpather  started business of Agar wood  in
Barpather in their homestead .  They collected raw agar wood from the forest Mahals  of  Karbi
Angong and Naga Hills  etc . Meanwhile, several enthusiastic and visionary people of Hojai
area  entered  in the trade  and started small-scale  distillation at Hojai.  A few years later , the
Agar trade & distillery  business also began in Naharani & other parts of Jorhat , Sibsagar
Districts of upper Assam,  utilizing  Agar wood  harvested from their own homegrown  Trees.
 At that period of time i,e in the late 60s , a group of enthusiastic people of Hojai involved in
taking lease of agar mahals in NEFA and Bhutan and collected agar from there and  brought to
Hojai  .  The Govt. of A used to lease Agarwood Mahals for a fixed period which continued till
1970 in  Assam, and by this time Agar trade developed as a cottage industry in the
un-organized sector  .
However,  from 1970 to 1980s the  entrepreneurs of Hojai & Silchar collected  Agarwood  from
the Mahalders of Manipur State ( under Forest Transit Pass ) which were processed & distilled
for oil  at  Hojai and Silchar  . These products were subsequently taken to Bombay ( Mumbai )
under Lieu T.P. issued by  Forest Deptt. Of Assam for sale . .

NEW DIMENSION TO AGAR TRADE DUE TO DISCOVERY OF WILD AGARWOOD IN THE
FAR EAST ASIAN COUNTRIES .
For a short while from 1970  to 1980  trade of agar  in Assam  survived somehow with the agar
from the state of Manipur but it came to a standstill since 1982 . However, there happened  a
sudden change in the trade which started with the discovery of Agar wood abumdantly  in the
far Eastern countries like Malaysia , Indonesia, Thiland , Combodia etc . Some prominent
business houses of Hojai Assam started importing agar wood from those countries as per Exim
Policy of Govt. of India after paying heavy import duties . It is pertinent to mention that in Hojai ,
Assam has good number of skilled agar processing workers as well as for Agar oil distilleries
and the employment was as good as 5000 people. The raw agar wood so imported were
re-exported after processing in their factory as finished products .

                    NATIONAL  SEMINAR on AGARWOOD PLANTATION
In November 2000 , AATMA organized a National level seminar on Scope & Dimension of Agar
(Aquilaria spp.) plantation in North Eastern Region at Administrative staff college ,Guwahati,
which was attended by several scientist from different parts of India and was presided over by
Dr. P.K. Hajra , Ex Director of the Botanical Survey of India . Several scientific reports on Agar
wood and its plantation and development submitted by the participant scientist are incorporated
in the proceedings of the seminar .    The seminar was an eye-opener to different entrepreneurs
who started commercial plantation in their private land in North Eastern states like Assam,
Nagaland, Tripura and Manipur etc. where it is a great success and are major source of raw
materials for Agar wood trade & Industry of the region now a days. Survey conducted jointly by
AATMA and  Assam Govt. reveals that there are more than crores of agar trees in private
cultivation in Upper Assam region , and other parts of the state commercially .  Similarly there
are reports that  wide spread private cultivation have flourished in Tripura and Nagaland as well
. It is worth mentioning that lakhs of people are involved in the cultivation agar trade as well.

CRITICAL ERA OF AGAR TRADE (Year 2001 to 2016)
In the year 2000 the Govt. of Assam framed Rules as “Assam Wood Based Industries (
Establishment and Regulation ) Rules, 2000 “ classifying  agar oil extracting unit as wood based
industries, these rules plunged Agar growers and traders into a period of deep uncertainties to
the industry and the trade .
Consequently , on June 26 ,2000, representative of the All Assam Agar Traders and Agaroil
Manufactures` (AATMA) met with the then Chief Minister of Assam. They submitted a
Memorandum urging Government  to exclude agar oil extraction  units from the purview of  the
these Rules, arguing that traders sourced their raw materials from private cultivations (patta land
or homesteads) and through legal Import from abroad .The memorandum further requested that
the agar wood industry be officially declared  an  agro-based industry. However, no concrete

action was taken at the time , leaving  the agar oil extraction industry without its hard earn
much-needed official recognition.
However , in 2001 the AATMA submitted fresh representation and memorandum  before the
Government of Assam, urging tthe exclusion Agar oil extracting units from the purview of
“Assam Wood Based Industries ( Establishment and Regulation ) Rules, 2000. This matter was
vigorously pursued multiple times.  Consequently, In December 2003, at the request of the then
Forest Minister,  AATMA  sponsored and participated in “ The Workshop on Agar Plantation and
the Rule of Joint Forest Management Committees of Assam,”  which was held in  Rabindra
Bhavan on 6th and 7th January 2004 .
Until that point,  Agar processing units were not govern by the aforementioned Rules.  Instead ,
these  units continued  to survive by  the import of agar wood  from abroad until 2005. This
arrangement required   verification of consignments by the concerned Divisional Forest
Officer(DFO) at the time of both  import and re-export to ensure compliance with   CITES
regulations . Unfortunately  October  2005 the then DFO  of  Hojai,  Shri Siv Kumar , IFS denied
the verification. This action , which was unlawful  and arbitrarily, was allegedly based on the
verbal instruction from the then Forest Minister of Assam. As a result,  around  Seven
Processing Units to suffered massive financial  losses because they were unable to re-export
their raw and processed Agar wood . This caused  severe damage to the local industry and a
significant  loss of foreign exchange earning .   Finding no alternative, several prominent
business houses shifted their operations (industry) to neighboring state like Meghalaya , as well
as Overseas.  Consequently , the prospects of a highly viable industry were severely crippled
and strangled within Assam.
As per CCF(T) letter No.FGT39/WBI/Amand/SecondaryWBI/06 dt.01,03. 2006, the Central
Empowered Committee  agreed to certain changes in Assam Wood Based Industries (
Establishment and Regulation ) Rules, 2000 “ and recommended to incorporate the Agarwood
industries in Secondary Wood Based Industry which could be set up outside designated
Industrial Area . But unfortunately the Assam Cabinet did not approve the recommendation .
Thus all efforts of AATMA to streamline the trade did not succeed due to the deaf ear and
negligence of the then Govt. of Assam .
 The office bearers of AATMA although disheartened with the negative approach of the Govt of
Assam did not sit idly and during the period 2005- 2016 continued to meet the then Chief
Minister/ Forest Minister as well as concerned forest deptt. authorities and submitted
memorandums for excluding agar industry from the purview of the above said Rules - 2000 . As
stated above the Forest Deptt. submitted several recommendations proposing to bring Agar
industries under the “ Secondary Wood Based Industry “ as approved by the Central High
Power Committee . “  These efforts did not yield desired result as yet and Agar Industry did not
receive any due recognition ,

Because the agar industry was never officially legalized or exempted under these rigid
rules—largely due to the persistent unwillingness of the then Forest Minister up until 2016—the
everyday reality for those on the ground became a nightmare. Routine transportation of agar
wood—even when legally harvested from private, cultivated lands—was treated as a criminal
act. Business personnel and local cultivators faced relentless harassment from both legal
authority and forest department officials. This hostile environment led to numerous unjustified

On Augast 24, 2016, an AATMA delegation met with  then Forest
Minister Smt. Pramila Rani Brahma, at the Hojai Forest circuit House . The delegation

Achivement
Following this on 19/07/2021, an AATMA delegation accompanied by   Mr. Sirajuddin Ajmal,
MLA, met with the Chief minister Assam, Shri Himanta Biswa Sharma, to submit a
memorandum apprising him of the ongoing problems face by Agar industries from last 21 years,
due to  Asaam wood Base Industries (Eastablishment & Regulation) Rule 2000. The chief
minister patiently listened to the delegation , acknowledged the memorandum , and assured
them he would work on resolving the matter
True to his assurance, the Chief Minister of Assam and his government took fresh , positive
approach to the long-standing , vexed issue .The association extends its sincere thanks to Sri
Himanta Biswa Sharma for enacting the Assam Wood Base Industries (Promotion &
Development) Rule 2022 on 12th. January 2023. Consequent upon the Rules  Agar Wood
Industries are now declared as Secondary Wood Based Industries, which can operate outside
the Industrial estate , successfully liberalizing   and simplifying the trade from the grip of strict
laws . These new  Rules will definitely pave the way for smooth trade and overall  development
of plantations .
